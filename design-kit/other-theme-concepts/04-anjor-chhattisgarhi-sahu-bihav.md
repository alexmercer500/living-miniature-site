# Theme 4: Anjor (अंजोर), Tel, Baati aur Bihav

*A Chhattisgarhi wedding theme for a Sahu family*

> **जइसे दीया म तेल अउ बाती, तइसे हमर दू परिवार।**
> *Like oil and wick in a diya, so are our two families. Apart, they wait. Together, they make light.*

**Feel:** A warm, colourful **Chhattisgarhi village wedding** that you walk through. Mud-walled homes with khapra-tiled roofs, a courtyard plastered with cow dung, rice-flour *chowk* patterns, bamboo *mandwa*, paddy fields, the village pond, palash flowers, kosa-silk gold and the glow of hundreds of diyas. The website follows the real Chhattisgarhi *nengs* (rituals) in order, so elders will recognise every step, and younger guests from outside Chhattisgarh will learn what each one means.

**Language:** Chhattisgarhi / Hindi first with an English toggle. Elders should feel this was made *for them*.

---

## The story idea

The whole site is about **light (अंजोर)**.

A diya needs three things: the **clay lamp**, the **oil (तेल)** and the **wick (बाती)**. On its own, oil is just oil and a wick is just thread. Together, in the hands of family, they become light.

- The **oil** and the **wick** are the bride's and groom's families.
- The **clay diya** is the elders and the samaj who hold them together.
- **Each neng** adds something: soil, oil, turmeric, blessings, and the flame grows a little brighter.
- By the end of the scroll, a single unlit diya has become a **whole village glowing with diyas**.

**Why it fits a Sahu family especially:** *Tel* (oil) has a special place in Sahu heritage. The community traditionally ran the *ghani* (oil press), and **Bhakt Mata Karma**, the community's revered saint, was born into a Sahu Teli family. In the most-told story about her, she lovingly fed Lord Jagannath **khichdi**, and the khichdi offering at Puri is remembered in her name. The wedding's own **Tel-Haldi** neng is literally "oil and turmeric". So the oil-lamp story is personal, not just pretty.

> ⚠️ **Family check:** Some families love referencing the ghani / oil-press heritage, while others prefer to keep it only devotional (Mata Karma). The oil-press chapter (Scene 3B) is **optional**; ask the elders.

**Story arc:**
Light the diya → Blessings (Ganesh ji, Mata Karma, kuldevi) → Oil + wick (names) → How the rishta happened (mangni-janchni) → The families (saga-sodar) → **Day 1: Mandwa, Chulmati, Tel-Haldi, Mayan** → **Day 2: Maur, Parchhan, Baraat, Parghani, Bhanwar, Tikawan** → **Day 3: Bidai, Dola Parchhan, Kankan-Maur** → Reception → Light a diya for the couple (RSVP) → The whole village glows.

## Visual direction

**Palette:** earth and festival colours of a Chhattisgarhi wedding.

| Role | Colour | Hex | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Background | Rice-flour white | `#FFF8EC` | Chowk patterns drawn with rice flour |
| Earth | Mud-wall clay | `#D9B38C` | Village house walls |
| Earth dark | Terracotta / khapra tile | `#A8502A` | Roof tiles, clay diya |
| Haldi | Turmeric yellow | `#F2B705` | Tel-Haldi |
| Wedding red | Sindoor / kumkum | `#C8102E` | Bhanwar, bride's saree |
| Fields | Paddy green | `#4E9F3D` | *Dhan ka katora*, the rice bowl of India |
| Festive pop | Lugra magenta | `#D6336C` | Bright Chhattisgarhi lugra sarees |
| Forest accent | Palash orange | `#F26B1D` | Palash (tesu) flowers blooming in wedding season |
| Gold | Kosa silk gold | `#C8A165` | Kosa silk, brass utensils (tikawan) |
| Night | Village-night indigo | `#1E1B4B` | Final diya scene |
| Flame | Diya glow | `#FF9F1C` | Everywhere light appears |

**Typography (Google Fonts, all support Devanagari):**
- Display: **Amita** (brush-calligraphy feel in both Devanagari and English) for names, chapter titles and big words like "अंजोर"
- Headings in Hindi/Chhattisgarhi: **Karma** (a Devanagari serif; the name is a sweet coincidence with Mata Karma)
- Body: **Mukta** (very readable on small phones, in Hindi and English)

**Art style:** Inspired by Chhattisgarhi folk art:
- **Bhitti chitra** (painted mud walls): figures in white and red on clay-coloured walls. Use this style for the love story.
- **Chowk / rangoli**: geometric rice-flour patterns, drawn line-by-line as you scroll (SVG animation). These act as section dividers.
- **Godna** (traditional tattoo motifs): dots, lines and simple geometric repeats for borders and the countdown.
- **Dhokra brass** (Bastar metal-casting style): small figures for the baraat band and dancers.
- A paper/mud texture over everything, so it feels handmade, not digital.

**Sound:** The local wedding baaja: **mohri** (shehnai-like), **dafda**, **nisan**, **timki**. Families call this band by different names (*gadwa / ganda baaja*), so ask what yours calls it. Use a soft folk tune for the opening. For the Tel-Haldi scene, a short recording of the family's women singing **"एक तेल चढ़गे…"** would be pure magic: record it on a phone at a pre-wedding gathering.

## Scroll flow, scene by scene

### Scene 0: दीया जलावव (Light the Diya), the gate screen
- **See:** A dark screen. In the centre, an **unlit clay diya** sits on a white rice-flour chowk pattern. Text: **जय जोहार** 🙏
- **Interaction:** A button: **"दीया जलावव · Light the diya"** (press and hold). As the guest holds, oil fills the diya and the wick soaks (this is the loading bar). On release, the **flame catches**, warm light spreads from the centre and the page opens. Below: 🎵 *Baaja ke sang* (with music) / *Bina awaaz* (silent).

### Scene 1: Aashirwad (the blessings)
- **See:** Three glowing niches in a mud wall (like the little *aala* shelves in village homes), each lit by a diya:
  - **Shri Ganesh**: ॥ श्री गणेशाय नमः ॥
  - **Bhakt Mata Karma**: shown with her pot of khichdi, Lord Jagannath's eyes in the background. ॥ जय माँ कर्मा ॥
  - **Kuldevi / Kuldevta**: your family deity (or Thakur Dev, Sheetla Maa, Mahamaya, whichever your family worships)
- **Animation:** Each niche lights up one after another as you scroll.
- **Copy:**
  > *माँ कर्मा के खिचड़ी म जइसे भगवान ल प्रेम मिलिस,*
  > *तइसे हमर बिहाव म आप मन के आशीर्वाद मिलय।*
  > *As Lord Jagannath found love in Mata Karma's khichdi, may this wedding find love in your blessings.*

### Scene 2: Tel aur Baati (the names)
- **See:** Big, close-up: **oil pours** into a clay diya from one side (bride's family name) and a **cotton wick** is twisted by hand on the other (groom's family name). As you scroll they meet inside the diya, the flame rises, and the names appear in its light.
- **Copy:**
  > *तेल अकेला रहय त बस तेल हे, बाती अकेला रहय त बस धागा।*
  > *दूनों मिलथें, तभे अंजोर होथे।*
  > *Alone, oil is just oil and a wick is just thread. Together, they make light.*
  >
  > **[Bride]** ✦ **[Groom]**
  > **शुभ बिहाव** · [Dates] · [Village / Town], Chhattisgarh

### Scene 3: Rishta (how it happened), painted on a mud wall
- **See:** A long **mud wall** of a village house. The love story is **painted on it in bhitti-chitra style** (white and red figures). As you scroll, the wall slides past and each painting "dries in", first wet and faint, then bold.
- **Panels (fill in your real story):**
  1. **दू गाँव, दू घर**: Two villages / towns: [Bride]'s house in [Place A], [Groom]'s in [Place B]. A quirk about each.
  2. **पहली भेंट**: First meeting. Where? A cousin's wedding, college, office, a Mata Karma Jayanti gathering, a phone call?
  3. **मंगनी-जँचनी**: The families visit, with a meal on the floor in leaf plates and the famous *saga-neti* talk, "neither rushed nor slow". *(Fun caption: "Questions were asked. Pedigrees were checked. Khurmi was eaten.")*
  4. **फलदान**: The engagement (*phaldan*): the bride receives clothes and jewellery from her new family; a measure of rice is given. The date is fixed.

### Scene 3B (optional): Ghani (the oil that lights the village)
- **See:** A traditional **wooden ghani (oil press)** turning slowly as you scroll; golden oil flowing into a brass pot.
- **Copy:**
  > *हमर पुरखा मन के घानी ले निकले तेल ह कतको घर म अंजोर करिस।*
  > *आज उही तेल ले हमर घर के नवा दीया जलत हे।*
  > *The oil from our ancestors' ghani lit many homes. Today, it lights a new one.*

### Scene 4: Saga-Sodar (the families)
- **See:** Two village homes side by side; their doors open as you scroll, revealing the families (illustrated or photo cutouts with a painted filter).
- **Copy:**
  > *स्वर्गीय [Grandparents] के आशीर्वाद ले*
  >
  > **[Bride]**: सुपुत्री श्रीमती [Mother] / श्री [Father] साहू, [Village/Town]
  > **[Groom]**: सुपुत्र श्रीमती [Mother] / श्री [Father] साहू, [Village/Town]
  >
  > *आप मन ल सादर नेवता हे*
  > *You are lovingly invited*

### 🌾 DAY 1: Tel ke Din (the Day of Oil)

**Transition:** A rice-flour **chowk pattern draws itself** across the screen, then the day's title: **पहला दिन · तेल**

| Neng | What guests see (animation) | What it means (one line, shown under the art) |
|---|---|---|
| **मड़वा (Mandwa)** | Bamboo poles **rise from the courtyard** as you scroll; mango/jamun leaves and the *mangrohan* (carved wooden figure) are tied on; the *kalash* lamp lights | *The bamboo canopy where every ritual will happen; the mangrohan wards off bad omens.* |
| **चुलमाटी (Chulmati)** | A procession of married women (*suvasin*) in bright lugra sarees walks to the **village pond**; one digs soil with a *sabbal*, with laughter and teasing songs in speech bubbles; they return carrying soil in a basket | *Sacred "virgin" soil is brought home, and the wedding has officially begun.* |
| **तेल-हरदी (Tel-Haldi)** | **Signature scene.** The bride/groom sits on a *peedha* under the mandwa. As the guest scrolls, **drops of oil and haldi** are offered one by one and a counter ticks: *एक तेल चढ़गे… दू तेल चढ़गे…*. The screen warms to turmeric yellow | *Oil and turmeric are lovingly applied, with the family's women singing the famous "Ek tel chadhge" song.* |
| **मायन / मायमौरी (Mayan)** | Behind a cloth screen, the **maternal family (mama's side)** arrives with gifts: clothes, money, utensils. Gift icons pop in | *The mother's family brings gifts; the song asks the ancestors to bless the wedding and forgive any mistakes.* |
| **हरदियाही / चिकट (Haldiyahi)** | **Playful moment:** turmeric splashes fly onto the *whole screen*, including the guest. Text: "आप घलो हरदी म रंग गेव! You've been haldi'd too!" | *Everyone under the mandwa gets smeared with turmeric. No one escapes.* |

**Event card for Day 1:** Date · Time (Chulmati in the evening, Tel-Haldi after) · Venue (home address + map) · Dress: **yellow / haldi colours** · 📍 Directions · 📅 Add to calendar

> 💡 **Two-house schedule:** In Chhattisgarhi weddings, Tel-Haldi and other nengs happen **separately at both homes**. Use **personalised links**: guests from the bride's side see the bride's home schedule and address, and guests from the groom's side see the groom's. Close family sees both.

### 🥁 DAY 2: Bihav ke Din (the Wedding Day)

**Transition:** Chowk pattern in red; title: **दूसर दिन · बिहाव**

| Neng | What guests see (animation) | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **नहडोरी · मौर · कंकन** | The groom gets his ritual bath, new clothes, the **maur** (wedding crown) placed on his head, and the **kankan** thread tied on his wrist; the maur sparkles | *The groom is bathed, dressed and crowned for the baraat.* |
| **परछन (Parchhan)** | Women circle a **kalash with a lit diya** around the groom; the circle of light traces on screen as you scroll | *Aarti to protect the groom from the evil eye, done when the baraat leaves and again when it returns.* |
| **बारात (Baraat)** | **Signature moment:** the baraat procession moves across the screen as you scroll: **mohri, dafda, nisan** players (dhokra-style brass figures), dancing baratis, the groom in his maur, fireworks | *The groom's side sets off with the baaja!* |
| **परघनी (Parghani)** | The bride's family at the gate; a priest welcomes the groom; flowers shower | *The bride's family welcomes the baraat at their door.* |
| **भड़ौनी + लालभाजी** | **Fun section:** speech bubbles of playful teasing; the bride's younger sister serves the groom **lal bhaji**. Interactive button: *"Will the groom finish the lal bhaji? 😄"* (tap for a funny answer) | *The bride's side sings cheeky teasing songs at the baratis; the saali serves red greens to the groom. It's all in good fun!* |
| **पाणिग्रहण (Panigrahan)** | Two hands close-up: the bride's hand placed in the groom's, tied with thread; the father's hands above giving blessing | *The bride's father places her hand in the groom's; a lifelong promise.* |
| **भाँवर (Bhanwar)** | **Signature scene.** The sacred fire from above. As the guest scrolls, the couple circles the fire **seven times**, and after each round the bride touches the **singholiya** (rice basket) with her foot and **rice scatters across the screen**. A small counter: भाँवर 1/7… 7/7 | *The seven rounds around the sacred fire. Now they are bound for life.* |
| **टिकावन (Tikawan)** | Gifts from relatives **shower in**: brass plates, utensils, jewellery, envelopes, all in kosa-gold | *Relatives offer gifts and blessings to the couple, each according to their love.* |

**Event cards for Day 2:** Baraat departure (groom's side) · Baraat arrival / Parghani (bride's side) · **Bhanwar muhurat** · Dinner (*jevanas*). Each has time, venue, map and calendar buttons. Dress: **traditional, reds and golds**.

### 🪔 DAY 3: Bidai aur Swagat (Farewell and Welcome)

**Transition:** The music softens. The colours dim to early-morning blue.

| Neng | What guests see (animation) | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **बिदा (Bidai)** | A quiet, emotional scene: the doli leaves the bride's village at dawn; the mother's hand lets go; handwritten text fades in slowly. **No jokes here.** | *The bride leaves her parents' home for her new one.* |
| **डोला परछन (Dola Parchhan)** | At the groom's door, his mother performs the welcome with *supa* (winnowing basket); the bride steps onto a **sieve filled with paddy**, leaving **paddy footprints** that walk into the house | *The groom's mother welcomes her new daughter home; Lakshmi enters with the bride.* |
| **कंकन-मौर (Kankan-Maur)** | **Mini-game for guests!** A brass *parat* of water with coins and a ring hidden inside. The guest taps the water to find the ring. Win and confetti bursts: *"You found it! Now let's see who wins between [Bride] and [Groom]…"* | *The couple's kankan threads are untied, and they compete to find a ring and coins in water, seven times. Whoever wins is said to rule the house!* |

**Reception / Swagat Samaroh** (if you are having one): an evening card with time, venue, map. Dress: formal / festive.

- **See:** A row of small diyas along a godna-pattern border. Numbers glow inside them.
- **Copy:** *"बिहाव म अब **42** दिन बाकी हे"* · *42 days to go*

### Scene 6: Ek Diya Aapke Naam (RSVP + blessing)
- **See:** A long row of **unlit diyas** along the mud wall at night.
- **Form:** Name · Number of guests (– / +) · Which days you'll attend · *"दूल्हा-दुल्हिन बर आशीर्वाद लिखव"* (write a blessing for the couple)
- **Animation on submit:** One diya in the row **lights up with the guest's name** under it. With a small database (Google Sheets / Supabase), the row grows as more guests reply, and the couple can scroll through **everyone's lamps and blessings** forever.

### Scene 7: Rasta (how to reach)
- **See:** A hand-drawn village/town map in folk style: pond, temple, both homes, the wedding venue, bus stand and railway station (e.g. Raipur / Bilaspur / Durg). Each icon taps through to Google Maps.
- Contacts: *"कुछु पूछना हे त फोन करव"*: [Name] (bride's side) · [Name] (groom's side), with Call / WhatsApp buttons.

### Scene 8: Anjor (the finale)
- **See:** The camera pulls back. **The whole village at night**: every house, the mandwa, the pond's edge, lined with glowing diyas (including the guests' RSVP diyas). The couple's diya burns brightest in the middle.
- **Copy:**
  > *एक दीया ले दू दीया, दू ले पूरा गाँव। आप मन आहू त अंजोर पूरा होही।*
  > *One diya lights two, two light the village. Come, and make the light complete.*
  >
  > **दर्शनाभिलाषी / स्वागतातुर:** [Family names, Sahu parivar]
  >
  > *नान्हे पहुना:* "मोर दीदी के बिहाव म जरूर आहू!" (cousins' names)
  >
  > **जय जोहार 🙏** · #[YourHashtag]
- Button: **🪔 फेर ले देखव · See it again**

## Signature interactions
1. **Hold to light the diya**: the opening
2. **"Ek tel chadhge…"**: oil drops counted as you scroll during Tel-Haldi
3. **"You've been haldi'd too!"**: turmeric splashed over the guest's screen
4. **Bhanwar with rice scattering** from the singholiya after each round
5. **Kankan-Maur ring-finding game**
6. **Your diya lights up in the village** when you RSVP

## Mobile notes
- Like the other themes, it's **vertical-first**: the village "walk" is a downward scroll. Only the baraat and the mud-wall story move sideways, and both are driven by normal scrolling (no swiping).
- Many guests may be on slower connections in smaller towns: keep the first screen (diya) **tiny**. The diya and flame can be SVG/CSS, almost 0 KB. Load village illustrations only as guests reach them.
- **Big Hindi text:** Devanagari needs a slightly larger size than English to read well. Use at least 18px body on phones.
- **Quick Info mode** ("सीधा जानकारी") is especially important here: elders in the village should be able to see *just* the dates, times and addresses in big text, and tap to call.

## ⚠️ Check with the elders before building
Chhattisgarhi customs **vary by village, district and family**. The names, order and who does what differ. Before finalising, sit with a dadi / nani / bua and confirm:

- [ ] Which nengs your family actually does, in what order, and on which days
- [ ] What your family calls them (e.g. Tel-Haldi vs Tel Chadhana; Parghani vs Pargauni)
- [ ] Which happen at the bride's home vs the groom's home
- [ ] Your kuldevi / kuldevta and how to show them respectfully
- [ ] Whether to include the ghani / oil-press reference (Scene 3B)
- [ ] All Chhattisgarhi lines in this file (I wrote them from standard Chhattisgarhi; get a native speaker in the family to polish the wording and spelling)
- [ ] Whether a recording of the family's women singing "Ek tel chadhge" can be used
