# Theme 3: Picture Abhi Baaki Hai, The Bollywood Blockbuster

> *"Two families proudly present… the biggest love story of the year. In cinemas (and banquet halls) this [Month]."*

**Feel:** A **90s hand-painted Bollywood film poster** meets a movie trailer. Loud colours, marquee lights, film-reel textures, dramatic zooms and dialogue cards. Funny, filmy and full of masala: the theme cousins will forward to *everyone*. Underneath the humour, the love story and blessings are still heartfelt.

---

## The story idea

Your wedding is a **blockbuster film**. The guest is in a single-screen cinema hall. The invitation plays like a movie, from the censor certificate to opening credits, trailer, cast, interval, song sequences, climax and the after-credits party.

**Choose your film title.** Pick a pun on a classic (or make your own):
- *Jab We Met* → **"Jab We Married"**
- *Kuch Kuch Hota Hai* → **"Ab Sab Kuch Hota Hai"**
- *Dilwale Dulhaniya Le Jayenge* → **"[Groom] Dulhaniya Le Jayega"**
- *Hum Aapke Hain Koun..!* → **"Hum Aapke Ho Gaye"**
- *Band Baaja Baaraat* → **"[Bride] Weds [Groom]: Band, Baaja & Full Baaraat"**
- Or an original: **"[Bride] Aur [Groom]: Ek Prem Kahani"**

> ⚠️ Use *puns and references only*. Don't use real film posters, stills or copyrighted songs. Commission original poster art, and use royalty-free / commissioned music in a filmy style.

**Story arc:** Censor certificate → Curtain opens → Shubh Aarambh (deity, as old reels began) → Opening credits → Trailer (love story) → Cast & crew (families) → Interval (countdown) → Song sequences (events) → Climax (wedding) → After-credits party (reception) → Box office (RSVP) → "The End… Picture abhi baaki hai".

## Visual direction

**Palette (poster paint):**

| Role | Colour | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Cinema dark | Velvet black-maroon | `#120A10` |
| Curtain | Cinema red | `#C1121F` |
| Hero colour | Hot filmy pink | `#FF1F7A` |
| Title | Saffron | `#FF8C00` |
| Highlights | Electric yellow | `#FFD60A` |
| Accent | Retro teal | `#00A6A6` |
| Marquee / foil | Gold | `#E6B655` |
| Ticket paper | Cream | `#FFF4DC` |

**Typography (Google Fonts):**
- Film title: **Rozha One** or **Bungee Shade** (big, dimensional, poster lettering)
- Marquee lights & tickets: **Bungee**
- Credits: **Teko** (tall condensed, like real film credits)
- Body: **Poppins**
- Hindi: **Rozha One** (it supports Devanagari too)

**Art style:** **Hand-painted Bollywood poster**: painterly faces with dramatic lighting, the couple in a classic pose (looking into the distance, hair blowing in the wind), bold outlined title lettering with a 3D drop shadow, and side-character portraits floating in circles. Plus film grain, light leaks, reel scratches and sprocket holes on the edges.

**Sound:** Projector whirr → a filmy orchestra sting → dhol. The famous "dramatic zoom" sound effect (*dhan! dhan! dhan!*) for comic beats.

## Scroll flow, scene by scene

### Scene 0: The Censor Certificate (gate screen)
- **See:** A replica of the old **CBFC film certificate** (the one shown before every Indian film), in black-and-white with a film-grain flicker.
- **Copy:**
  > **Certificate: U** (Universal: suitable for all relatives, including Fufaji)
  > Title: **"Jab We Married"**
  > Length: 3 days of celebrations
  > Certified by: **Both Mummies** ✅
  > Language: Hindi, English & loud Punjabi music
- **Interaction:** **"🎬 Start the show (with sound)"** / **"Watch on mute"**. The certificate burns out like old film, and the **red velvet curtain** pulls open.

### Scene 1: Shubh Aarambh
- **See:** Like old film reels that began with a prayer: Ganesh ji in a golden glow, a diya, and a single "om" sound.
- **Copy:**
  > ॥ श्री गणेशाय नमः ॥
  > *Every great film begins with a blessing.*

### Scene 2: Opening Credits (the poster reveal)
- **See:** The **full film poster**: the couple painted in classic poster style, with the title in huge 3D letters.
- **Animation:** Credits roll **over** the poster as you scroll, each line fading in like a real film opening. The poster slowly zooms (Ken Burns effect).
- **Copy:**
  > **[Bride's family] & [Groom's family] Productions**
  > *present*
  >
  > *A* **[Bride] × [Groom]** *film*
  >
  > **Story, Screenplay & Dialogues:** Destiny
  > **Casting Director:** [The friend / aunt / app who introduced you]
  > **Music:** Dhol Wale Bhaiya
  > **Costume Design:** Mummy (no arguments accepted)
  > **Action Director:** Chachi on the dance floor
  > **Catering:** The real hero
  >
  > # JAB WE MARRIED
  > *Releasing [Date] · [City]*

### Scene 3: The Trailer (the love story)
- **See:** A **film strip** runs down the screen (sprocket holes on both sides). Each frame is a "scene" from your story: an illustration plus a dialogue card in classic Bollywood subtitle style (yellow text, black outline).
- **Animation:** Each frame **snaps to fill the screen** as you scroll. On dramatic moments, a **triple zoom** hits the character's face (*dhan! dhan! dhan!*, the beloved TV-serial meme).
- **Scenes (fill with your real story):**
  1. **"Scene 1: Pehli Mulaqat"** · *[Place, year]*. Dialogue: *"Excuse me… yeh seat khaali hai?"* (whatever really happened)
  2. **"Scene 2: Takraar"** (the first argument / misunderstanding). Dramatic zoom on [Bride]'s face.
  3. **"Scene 3: Palat"**: The DDLJ moment. *"If she loves him… she'll turn around. Palat… palat… **palat!**"* ([Bride] or [Groom] turns as the guest scrolls: **interactive moment**, scroll controls the turn.)
  4. **"Scene 4: Rishta Pakka"**: Both families on a sofa, a plate of laddoos, Papa's stern face… then a smile. *"Mujhe yeh rishta manzoor hai."*

### Scene 4: Cast & Crew (the families)
- **See:** A classic film "star cast" layout: circular portraits (illustrated or photos with a painted filter).
- **Copy:**
  > **Starring**
  > **[Bride]** as *The Bride* · **[Groom]** as *The Groom*
  >
  > **Produced by** (with love and a large budget)
  > Smt. [Mother] & Shri [Father] · Smt. [Mother] & Shri [Father]
  >
  > **With the blessings of** Late Shri [Grandfather] & Late Smt. [Grandmother]
  >
  > **Special Appearance:** Dadi, Nani & all the elders
  > **Introducing:** [Siblings] as *The Chaotic Siblings*
  > **Friendly Appearance:** [The friend gang]

### Scene 5: INTERVAL (the countdown)
- **See:** The screen goes to the classic **"INTERVAL"** card. The cinema lights come up and a **samosa and cold-drink** illustration appears.
- **Copy:**
  > **INTERVAL**
  > *Main feature begins in* **42 days 6 hrs 13 min**
  > *Go grab a samosa. 🥟*

### Scene 6: Song Sequences (the events)
Each event is a **"song"** from the film's soundtrack, with its own set, colours and choreography. Each song opens with a vinyl/cassette label ("Track 01") and ends with an event card (date, time, venue, dress code, **📍 Directions**, **📅 Add to calendar**).

| Track | Event | Filmy set | What animates | Card line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Haldi** | The famous yellow **mustard field** (a nod to the DDLJ fields) | Mustard flowers sway; the couple runs toward each other in slow motion as you scroll | *"Song: 'Peeli Peeli Haldi'"* · Dress: yellow |
| 02 | **Mehendi** | Garden swing (*jhoola*) with fairy lights | Mehendi draws itself on a hand; **easter egg:** find the hidden names for a "Bonus Scene" | *"Song: 'Mehendi Laga Ke Rakhna, Phone Side Mein Rakhna'"* · Dress: green / floral |
| 03 | **Sangeet** | A **disco stage**: mirror ball, coloured lights, spotlight | Disco ball spins; spotlights sweep with scroll; silhouettes do the hook step | *"Song: 'Uncle-ji Ka Dance Number'"* · Dress: glam / Indo-western |
| 04 | **Baraat** | The street: horse, band-baaja, fireworks | Dhol players bounce; baraatis do the naagin dance; the horse trots across | *"Song: 'Band, Baaja, Full Baaraat'"* |
| 05 | **Wedding: THE CLIMAX** | Mandap under a starry sky, rose petals in slow motion | The music quietens. Seven pheras appear as seven frames of film, each with one vow | **Shubh Vivah** · Muhurat: [time] · Dress: traditional |
| 06 | **Reception: AFTER-CREDITS PARTY** | A premiere night: red carpet, paparazzi flashes | Camera flashes pop as you scroll; star walk-in | *"Red carpet entry. Paparazzi = your cousins."* · Dress: formal / black-tie |

> **Tone tip:** keep the Haldi, Mehendi, Sangeet and Baraat funny; let **the Climax (wedding)** turn soft and emotional: slower music, gentle wording, no jokes. That contrast is what makes it touching.

### Scene 7: Box Office (RSVP)
- **See:** An old **single-screen ticket counter** with a little window and a grumpy-cute ticket clerk (illustrated).
- **Form:** Name · **"How many seats?"** (– / +) · **"Which shows?"** (events) · Dialogue for the couple (their message)
- **Animation on submit:** A **cinema ticket prints** out of the counter slot: cream paper, perforated edge, the guest's name, seat count, events and a "Housefull" stamp. The guest can **download / share** the ticket image on WhatsApp. (Free promotion for your wedding!)

### Scene 8: Travel & Stay ("Theatre Locations")
- Styled like an old newspaper **film listings ad**: *"Now showing at [Venue], [City]. Shows: 12, 13, 14 Feb."* Tap to open maps.
- Contacts: *"For tickets & enquiries: [Name] (bride's side) · [Name] (groom's side)"*, with Call / WhatsApp buttons.

### Scene 9: The End
- **See:** A hand-painted **"THE END"** card... then it flickers, and a line from Om Shanti Om appears.
- **Copy:**
  > *…aur phir woh hamesha khushi khushi rehne lage.*
  >
  > # The End?
  > ### *Picture abhi baaki hai, mere dost.* 🎬
  >
  > *Your presence will make our film a superhit.*
  > *आपकी उपस्थिति प्रार्थनीय है*
  >
  > **Swagatotsuk:** [Family names]
  > *Nanhe mehmaan:* "Mere chachu ki shaadi mein zaroor aana!" (cousins' names)
  >
  > #[YourHashtag]
- **"Coming Soon" poster teaser at the very bottom:** *"Sequel: Happily Ever After. Releasing every day after [Date]."*

## Signature interactions
1. **The censor certificate** ("Certified by: Both Mummies")
2. **The curtain opening** and credits rolling over the poster
3. **"Palat… palat… palat!"**, where your scroll makes them turn around
4. **Dramatic triple-zoom** on comic moments
5. **Printing a cinema ticket** as the RSVP, then sharing it on WhatsApp

## Mobile notes
- A film strip is naturally vertical, so the trailer works perfectly as a phone scroll.
- Make the poster in **two crops**: portrait 9:16 for phones (the poster *is* the screen) and wide for desktop.
- Keep dialogue cards big: subtitle text at least 18px on phones.
- The downloadable ticket should be a **9:16 image** so it fits WhatsApp status / Instagram stories.
